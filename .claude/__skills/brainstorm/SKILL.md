# Brainstorm

Phases run in order. Diverge is most of the session.

## 1. Frame

Skip where the request already answers these. Otherwise ask one question, and only
where the answer changes what you generate.

- What is being decided or created.
- Which constraints are hard, and which are assumptions worth challenging.
- What good looks like: one winning idea, a map of the space, or a check on a
  direction they already hold.

## 2. Diverge

Loop:

1. Generate a batch of 5–8 ideas.
2. End the turn. Ask what pulls at them and what is missing.
3. Set the next batch's angle from their reaction. "None of these" is a steer.

Repeat until new ideas echo old ones.

Each batch:

- Each idea is a short name plus one or two sentences.
- Draw from three or more angles, changing them between batches.
- Carry a workhorse — the obvious solid answer belongs on the table.
- Carry one idea you think is too ambitious or probably wrong.

Angles: straightforward · inversion · analogy · constraint game · persona shift · recombination

Conduct:

- Hold judgement until Converge.
- When the user floats an idea, extend it or build its strongest version.
- Treat half-formed comments as prompts.
- When the user critiques an idea, log the concern in one line and continue.
- Every third batch, restate the running list.

## 3. Cluster

Trigger: ideas start echoing.

- Group into 3–5 named themes.
- Name the region left unexplored, and offer one batch there.
- Surface tensions in what they favoured — "your shortlist trades simplicity for power".

## 4. Converge

Enter on request: "which should I pick", "that's enough", "what do you think".
Open with "switching hats."

- Name 2–4 candidates.
- Judge against the Frame criteria rather than generic pros and cons.
- Give each a reason and a weakness, including ideas they are attached to.
- Recommend one. Where the call rests on their taste or risk appetite, state the
  trade-off and recommend anyway.

## 5. Capture

Full list grouped by theme, shortlist marked, open questions and next steps.

## Anti-patterns

- One exhaustive list in place of a dialogue.
- Ranking or naming a favourite during Diverge.
- Framing questions until the energy dies.
- A batch of only safe ideas, or only clever ones.
- Accepting the framing when the framing is the problem — they asked for a faster
  horse; the goal may be the commute.
- Losing early ideas because nothing was written down.
